# The hidden and the revealed: occultism, psyops, and power from Hollywood to Langley

**The intersection of occult practice, psychological operations, intelligence agencies, and entertainment is not a conspiracy theory — it is a documented historical phenomenon with named participants, declassified files, and court records.** Michael Aquino simultaneously held a Top Secret clearance and led an occult order. The CIA spent $20 million investigating psychic phenomena over 23 years. A JPL co-founder performed Thelemic sex magic with the future founder of Scientology. Hollywood manufactured occult mystique as a marketing strategy before the medium was two decades old. These facts are archival, not speculative. What makes this territory treacherous is that genuine documented history sits adjacent to an enormous body of mythology, self-serving fabrication, and unfalsifiable conspiracy theory — often produced by the same people. This report traces the documented record across five threads, flagging where evidence is strong, where it thins, and where trails go deliberately cold.

## Thread 1: Michael Aquino — the man who wrote PSYOP doctrine and founded a temple

### The military career

Michael A. Aquino Jr. (1946–2019) earned a B.A. in political science from UC Santa Barbara in 1968 as a Distinguished Military Graduate, receiving a Regular Army commission. He served in Vietnam as a psychological operations officer, deploying loudspeaker-equipped helicopters to broadcast psychologically disorienting audio over enemy positions. One documented operation exploited Vietnamese-Buddhist burial customs: a recording of funeral music transitioning into screams of a soldier "dragged away by demons," broadcast from above cloud cover during thunderstorms. He received the Bronze Star, Air Medal, and Republic of Vietnam Gallantry Cross.

After Vietnam, Aquino's career trajectory is documented through court records and his own CV: he served as Research & Analysis Team Leader at the 7th PSYOP Group at the Presidio of San Francisco, held a reserve attaché position at the **Defense Intelligence Agency** in 1981, attended the Defense Intelligence College and Foreign Service Institute, and completed the Industrial College of the Armed Forces at National Defense University. His positions would have required at minimum a **Top Secret/SCI clearance** — standard for DIA personnel — though the specific clearance level has not been independently confirmed from declassified primary documents. He retired as Lieutenant Colonel in 1994.

### MindWar: what the paper actually says

"From PSYOP to MindWar: The Psychology of Victory" (1980), co-authored with Colonel Paul E. Vallely (Commander, 7th PSYOP Group), was an unofficial internal "talking paper" — not classified, not formal doctrine, not an official policy document. Vallely circulated it to stimulate dialogue within PSYOP commands. Its core argument was radical: **psychological operations should become the primary instrument of warfare**, not a force-multiplier for conventional arms. "The advantage of MindWar is that it conducts wars in nonlethal, noninjurious, and nondestructive ways. Essentially you overwhelm your enemy with argument."

The paper's most controversial dimension is its stance on targeting friendly populations. It explicitly states: **"MindWar must target all participants if it is to be effective. It must not only weaken the enemy; it must strengthen the United States."** It then acknowledges that existing law prohibits PSYOP units from targeting American citizens, but argues this prohibition rests on the "presumption that 'propaganda' is necessarily a lie" — and since MindWar "always speaks the truth," it would be something categorically different. Critics have noted the rhetorical sleight of hand: Aquino simultaneously acknowledges the legal prohibition while arguing for its circumvention through semantic redefinition.

Three extended footnotes address technological enhancement of psychological operations. The paper references atmospheric electromagnetic activity and its effects on human cognition, air ionization (negative ions enhancing alertness, positive ions inducing drowsiness), and most notably **Extremely Low Frequency (ELF) waves**: "ELF-waves are not normally noticed by the unaided senses, yet their resonant effect upon the human body has been connected to both physiological disorders and emotional distortion. Infrasound could be used tactically... and it could be used in conjunction with media broadcasts as well."

MindWar was never adopted as official doctrine. But in his 2003 introduction, Aquino noted that Gulf War I and the 2003 Iraq invasion both applied MindWar principles — including journalist embedding, which "inevitably channel[ed] their perspectives and perceptions." Some of its core concepts (information dominance, media-centric warfare, perception management) have clear parallels in later military doctrine on information operations, though no direct doctrinal lineage has been established.

### The occult career and the split with LaVey

Aquino joined the Church of Satan in 1969 after encountering an advertisement in the *Berkeley Barb*. During Vietnam, he corresponded with Anton LaVey. By 1973, he held the rank of Magister Templi IV° — the highest achievable rank below LaVey's own — serving as editor of the Church newsletter *The Cloven Hoof* and sitting on the governing Council of Nine. He was effectively LaVey's right-hand man.

The 1975 split involved multiple fault lines. LaVey had begun **selling priesthoods for cash**, which Aquino viewed as betraying meritocratic principles. More fundamentally, the two men held irreconcilable metaphysical positions: LaVey was essentially an atheist who treated Satan as a symbolic archetype; **Aquino was a theist who believed in a literal preternatural entity**. On the North Solstice of 1975, Aquino performed a ritual and produced *The Book of Coming Forth by Night* — which he described as received through a process resembling automatic writing — in which Set (the Egyptian deity) proclaimed the beginning of a new Aeon and raised Aquino to the rank of Magus V°. He took approximately 100 followers (LaVey dismissively claimed only 29) to found the Temple of Set.

The Temple of Set taught that Set gave humanity the "Black Flame" — a questioning intellect providing "isolate self-consciousness" distinguishing humans from animals. The goal was **Xeper** (Egyptian: "I Have Come Into Being"), a process of radical self-transformation toward self-deification. The organization peaked at roughly **500 members** around 1984, maintained a six-degree initiatory system, and operated specialized internal orders including the Order of the Trapezoid. It continues to exist with a low public profile.

### The Wewelsburg Working

On October 19, 1982, while in Europe for NATO-related military duties, Aquino performed a ritual he called "The Wewelsburg Working" in the Hall of the Dead (Walhalla) in the North Tower of Wewelsburg Castle — the castle Heinrich Himmler had intended as a ceremonial center for the SS. The castle was then functioning as a youth hostel and small museum. Aquino's stated purpose was "to summon the Powers of Darkness at their most powerful locus" and to reconstitute the **Order of the Trapezoid** as a formal sub-order of the Temple of Set.

Aquino's relationship to Nazi esotericism was what historian Nevill Drury described as a conviction that "the Nazis were able to summon an extraordinary psychic force which was misdirected — but need not have been." He had published an article titled "That Other Black Order" in *The Cloven Hoof* in 1972, demonstrating longstanding interest in SS mysticism. True-crime reporter Michael Newton described Aquino's public stance on the Reich as "deliberately ambiguous."

### The Presidio allegations

The Presidio case must be handled with particular epistemological care. **What is documented from court records** (*Aquino v. Stone*, 957 F.2d 139, 4th Cir. 1992) and investigative journalism: In November 1986, investigations began into Gary Hambright, a civilian daycare worker at the Presidio Army Base, for child sexual abuse. In August 1987, the case expanded when Captain Larry Adams-Thompson's three-year-old daughter identified Aquino as "Mikey" — a name she had used in earlier therapy sessions. Investigators raided the Aquinos' residence, seizing videotapes, photographs, and costume materials.

**Documented physical evidence**: at least four children (ages 3–7) tested positive for chlamydia. More than 60 children were eventually involved in allegations. **Legal outcome**: Aquino was **never formally charged** by either civilian or military authorities. SFPD discontinued its investigation in September 1988 citing insufficient evidence. However, the Army CID **refused to remove Aquino's name** from its investigation report, stating "the evidence of alibi offered by LTC Aquino [was] not persuasive." The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals upheld this finding, ruling there was "sufficient evidence from which the Army decision maker could determine that probable cause existed."

The case exists in genuine epistemological limbo. It occurred during the nationwide "Satanic Ritual Abuse" moral panic, when numerous daycare centers faced allegations later found unfounded (McMartin, etc.), and interviewing techniques of the era have been heavily criticized. But the physical evidence (chlamydia in children) is documented and real. Aquino maintained his innocence until death, publishing *Extreme Prejudice: The Presidio "Satanic Abuse" Scam*. **Neither guilt nor innocence was conclusively established.**

### The conceptual bridge: Lesser Black Magic and PSYOP doctrine

The most analytically significant aspect of Aquino's career is the parallel between his occult taxonomy and military doctrine. His concept of **Lesser Black Magic** — "impelling things in the objective universe to perform desired acts by using obscure physical or behavioral laws," into which he explicitly placed politics and propaganda — maps remarkably onto PSYOP principles: systematic study of target psychology, manipulation of perceptions through crafted messaging, use of environmental factors to enhance suggestibility, and strategic application of influence without the target's awareness. **Greater Black Magic** — transformation of the self through ritual — maps onto the operator's own training and initiation.

Whether the occult practice informed the military doctrine or vice versa cannot be determined. The chronological overlap (Aquino developed both simultaneously throughout the 1970s–80s) makes disentangling causation impossible. What is documented is that he explicitly referenced "the esoteric arts of antiquity" alongside science and psychology in his 2013 expanded *MindWar* book.

## Thread 2: Anton LaVey — the showman and the substance behind the spectacle

### The documented man vs. the constructed legend

Anton LaVey (born **Howard Stanton Levey**, April 11, 1930, Chicago) was perhaps the most accomplished self-mythologizer in American religious history. Pulitzer Prize–winning journalist Lawrence Wright conducted the most rigorous independent investigation (Rolling Stone, 1991), and LaVey's own estranged daughter Zeena Schreck published corroborating findings in 1998. Most recently, Doug Brod's *Born with a Tail* (2024, Hachette) drew on 50+ interviews and archival research.

The results are damning for LaVey's autobiography. He claimed to have joined the Clyde Beatty Circus at 16 — **no evidence found** in circus route books. He claimed an affair with Marilyn Monroe — **debunked** by the manager of the theater where Monroe allegedly danced. He claimed to have played oboe in the San Francisco Ballet Orchestra — **no evidence found**. He claimed to have worked as an SFPD crime scene photographer — SFPD stated "no one named Howard or Anton LaVey or Levey ever worked for the force." His grandmother was Ukrainian, not "a Gypsy from Transylvania." The Black House was his parents' home, not a former brothel.

What *was* real: LaVey was a genuinely talented keyboardist (well-attested by multiple sources), did play organ at the Lost Weekend nightclub and San Francisco Civic Auditorium, and possessed undeniable charisma and showmanship. Wright's elegant assessment: "Anton LaVey is an imaginary creation, as real, and as false, as a soap-opera character or a comic-book anti-hero." Brod called him "one part pope, nine parts P.T. Barnum."

### The Hollywood connections that are real

**Sammy Davis Jr.** — documented in Davis's own 1989 autobiography and corroborated by a 2024 Rolling Stone investigation. Davis attended Church of Satan gatherings from approximately 1968–1974, wore a painted red fingernail (a Church signifier), and received the honorary title "Warlock II." He described his introduction through actors at The Factory nightclub and characterized events as "dungeons and dragons and debauchery." He departed around 1974 calling it "a short-lived interest."

**Jayne Mansfield** — documented through extensive photographic evidence (German paparazzo Walter Fischer, published in *California Infernal*, 2017). She visited the Black House in October 1966, received a medallion, and was dubbed "High Priestess." The nature of the relationship was mutually advantageous publicity. The famous "curse" story — LaVey allegedly cursing Mansfield's boyfriend Sam Brody, who then died alongside her in a June 1967 car accident — comes primarily from Church of Satan sources. The accident was a mundane rear-ending of a tractor-trailer in darkness.

**Kenneth Anger** — documented longtime friend, part of LaVey's pre-Church "Magic Circle." LaVey appeared in Anger's *Invocation of My Demon Brother* (1969). Anger briefly served as a Church of Satan priest.

LaVey's most persistent Hollywood claim — that he served as **technical advisor on *Rosemary's Baby* and played the Devil** — is **thoroughly debunked**. Producer Gene Gutowski stated LaVey never met Polanski. The Devil was played by actor Clay Tanner, confirmed by Paramount's cast records and on-set photographs. Even LaVey's former son-in-law Nikolas Schreck, himself a self-described Satanist, called this a fabrication. LaVey's only actual connection was appearing at the San Francisco premiere as a promotional gimmick. He did genuinely serve as technical advisor on the completely different, low-budget *The Devil's Rain* (1975).

### The Satanic Bible: a derivative but influential text

Published by Avon Books in 1969 (commissioned after the success of *Rosemary's Baby*), *The Satanic Bible* has sold over **600,000 copies** across 30+ printings — far exceeding actual Church membership, which never surpassed roughly 2,000. Its philosophical sources are well-documented: Ayn Rand's ethical egoism (LaVey himself stated "LaVeyan Satanism was just Ayn Rand's philosophy, with ceremony and ritual added"), Nietzsche's master-slave morality, and substantial sections copied nearly verbatim from **Ragnar Redbeard's** *Might Is Right* (1896) — a social Darwinist tract from which LaVey edited out the explicit racism and antisemitism. Enochian keys were adapted from Crowley with Satanic substitutions.

The Church's cultural function was dual: genuine philosophical framework (atheistic Satanism treating rituals as psychological psychodramas) and deliberate media spectacle. LaVey staged the first public Satanic wedding, baptism, and funeral, all designed for maximum press coverage. He appeared on *The Tonight Show*, *Donahue*, and was covered by *Time*, *Newsweek*, and *Cosmopolitan*. Sociologist James R. Lewis found that 60% of Church members viewed Satan as symbol rather than entity, and 20% cited *The Satanic Bible* itself as their primary attraction.

### Intelligence connections: what the record shows

FBI files on LaVey have been released via FOIA (approximately 55 pages, partially redacted). The most substantive entry documents a 1980 investigation into an alleged plot to assassinate Senator Ted Kennedy — which FBI and Secret Service quickly determined LaVey had no involvement in. During the investigation, agents reported LaVey's "interest in the Church of Satan is strictly from a monetary point of view." The files also contain bomb threats *against* the Church and foreign law enforcement inquiries.

**No documented direct connection between LaVey and CIA or military intelligence has been established.** Claims to this effect come from conspiracy-oriented sources lacking primary documentation. The documented geographic overlap between LaVey's San Francisco milieu and CIA operations (MK-ULTRA, the counterculture penetration programs) is real but does not constitute organizational connection.

## Thread 3: Occult imagery as Hollywood's original technology

### Theda Bara and the invention of manufactured mystique

The entertainment industry's relationship to the occult begins not with genuine belief but with **marketing**. Theodosia Burr Goodman (1885–1955) — daughter of Bernard Goodman, a Jewish tailor from Cincinnati, and Pauline de Coppett, born in Switzerland — was transformed by Fox Film Corporation publicists Al Selig and John Goldfrap into "Theda Bara," allegedly born "in the shadow of the Sphinx," whose name was an anagram of "Arab Death." In reality, "Theda" was her childhood nickname and "Bara" derived from her maternal grandfather's name.

The fabrication was executed with extraordinary sophistication for the 1910s. Fox staged a Chicago hotel press conference filled with lilies, crystal balls, a python in a gilded cage, and hired a phrenologist to publicly declare Bara had "the characteristics of a vampire and a sorceress." Her contract reportedly required veils in public and nocturnal outings only. She was photographed with skulls, snakes, and skeletons. Film titles were chosen accordingly: *Sin*, *Destruction*, *The Serpent*, *Salome*. The word **"vamp" entered the English language** because of her.

Between 1915 and 1919, Bara starred in over 40 films and earned $4,000 per week — behind only Chaplin and Pickford. In private, biographer Eve Golden documented, she was "a quiet, bookish woman" with "no problems with drugs or alcohol." Most of her films were destroyed in the 1937 Fox vault fire. The Bara phenomenon demonstrates that **Hollywood understood occult/exotic imagery as commercially potent marketing technology before the medium was two decades old** — and that the distance between the manufactured persona and the real person was already absolute.

### Jack Parsons: the documented nexus of rocketry, occultism, and science fiction

Jack Parsons (1914–1952) represents the most extraordinary documented case of simultaneous participation in military-industrial technology and active occult practice. He co-founded both the **Jet Propulsion Laboratory** and **Aerojet Engineering Corporation**, invented the first castable composite solid rocket propellant (foundational technology for the Space Shuttle boosters and Minuteman missiles), and has a lunar crater named after him. He had no college degree.

Parsons discovered Thelema through reading Aleister Crowley, joined the O.T.O.'s **Agape Lodge** in Pasadena around 1939, and became its head. He turned his mansion at 1003 South Orange Grove Avenue ("The Parsonage") into a bohemian commune, reportedly chanting Crowley's "Hymn to Pan" before rocket test launches. His social circle overlapped with the early science fiction community: he was friends with Robert Heinlein, attended meetings at Heinlein's Laurel Canyon home, and knew a teenage Ray Bradbury through the Los Angeles Science Fantasy Society. **Rocketry, occultism, and science fiction were not separate worlds in 1940s Pasadena — they were overlapping communities with the Parsonage as a literal nexus point.**

The **Babalon Working** (January–March 1946) is documented through Parsons' letters, Crowley's correspondence, and a peer-reviewed academic analysis by Henrik Bogdan in *Numen* (2016). L. Ron Hubbard moved into the Parsonage in August 1945; Parsons wrote to Crowley that Hubbard was "the most Thelemic person I have ever met." Hubbard served as "Scribe" during rituals involving Enochian magic. Upon the arrival of Marjorie Cameron — a former Navy WAVE — Parsons believed her to be the "elemental" woman he had invoked. Parsons then performed sex magic rituals with Cameron while Hubbard continued as assistant, and believed he received *Liber 49* via psychographic transmission.

**Crowley's reaction is documented**: "fairly frantic when I contemplate the idiocy of these louts!" The Church of Scientology later claimed (1969) that Hubbard had been "sent in" by the U.S. government to "break up Black Magic in America." This claim is contradicted by court testimony from Gerry Armstrong, Scientology's own former archivist, who stated under oath that he found the Naval Intelligence story was authored by Hubbard himself and that "Mr. Hubbard was not working for Naval intelligence; was, in fact, connected to the OTO." After the Working, Hubbard defrauded Parsons of approximately **$20,000** and absconded with his ex-girlfriend Sara Northrup.

Parsons died on June 17, 1952, in an explosion in his home laboratory while preparing pyrotechnics for a film industry rush order. His right forearm was severed and his legs and left arm broken. He was found conscious; his reported last words were "But I'm not finished yet." The official finding was accidental dropping of fulminate of mercury. Colleagues called him "extremely safety-conscious." His mother took a fatal overdose of barbiturates upon hearing the news. The circumstances remain genuinely ambiguous.

### Kenneth Anger: film as ritual, gossip as mythology

Kenneth Anger (1927–2023) made experimental films that were simultaneously sincere Thelemic ritual practice and genuinely influential avant-garde cinema. Martin Scorsese credited Anger with inspiring his own use of popular music in films. *Scorpio Rising* (1963) pioneered the music-video aesthetic decades before MTV.

Anger's *Hollywood Babylon* (1959/1965), which sold **two million copies**, has been systematically fact-checked by film historian Karina Longworth across 19 podcast episodes. The verdict: "many of Anger's gossipy, falsity-ridden retellings merely perpetuated inaccuracies" drawing on "racist, sexist, or homophobic stereotypes." Some stories were true and already known; others (Clara Bow sleeping with "an entire college football team") were fabricated. **It is an unreliable source that should never be used as primary history**, though it remains culturally significant as a camp artifact.

The Bobby Beausoleil connection provides the most chilling documented link between Anger's world and the Manson murders. **The timeline is established**: Beausoleil was cast as Lucifer in *Lucifer Rising* and lived with Anger in San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury in 1966–67. After a falling out with Anger in 1967, Beausoleil drifted to the Manson Family. On July 25, 1969, he murdered Gary Hinman under Manson's orders. The Tate-LaBianca murders followed two weeks later. Beausoleil's involvement with Anger **preceded** his involvement with Manson. He later composed the film's final soundtrack from prison with fellow inmates.

### Jay Dyer's "Esoteric Hollywood" — where legitimate criticism becomes unfalsifiable theory

Jay Dyer holds a B.A. in philosophy and runs JaysAnalysis.com. His *Esoteric Hollywood* (2016, published by Trine Day, a small conspiracy-oriented press) argues that Hollywood functions as "MK-Ultra culture creation" — a mass psychological operation embedding deliberate occult symbolism for social engineering purposes.

Dyer's work sits at an interesting boundary. **Some of his foundational premises are documented fact**: MK-ULTRA was real; the Pentagon's entertainment liaison office exists and shapes scripts; the CIA has influenced Hollywood productions (documented in Matthew Alford's *Reel Power* and Tricia Jenkins' *The CIA in Hollywood*); directors like Kubrick demonstrably embedded intentional symbolism. But Dyer's thesis makes **enormous inferential leaps** — from "Hollywood contains symbolism" and "intelligence agencies have influenced media" to "Hollywood systematically functions as an MK-Ultra mass mind control operation." The interpretive method tends toward unfalsifiability: any symbol can be decoded as "hidden meaning," making the framework impossible to test or disprove. No mainstream film scholars or peer-reviewed academic sources have engaged with or cited his work.

## Thread 4: The documented occult-intelligence nexus

### MK-ULTRA: what survived the shredder

MK-ULTRA (1953–1973) is the bedrock documented case. CIA Director Richard Helms ordered all files destroyed in 1973; approximately **20,000 pages survived** only because they had been incorrectly stored in a financial records building rather than the project files location. They were discovered via a 1977 FOIA request by journalist John Marks. These documents, supplemented by the 1975 Church Committee hearings and the 1977 Senate Select Committee hearings, reveal **149 documented subprojects across 80 institutions**: 44 colleges and universities, 15 research foundations or pharmaceutical companies, 12 hospitals and clinics, involving 185 identified non-government researchers.

Sidney Gottlieb headed the program. His recently fully declassified Church Committee testimony (published by the National Security Archive, October 2025) reveals he acknowledged the program had "as many failures as successes" and concluded "the money expended, the effort expended, the security risk involved... it was probably not a high pay-off program." The program funded research into LSD, mescaline, hypnosis, sensory deprivation, verbal and sexual abuse, and other methods of behavioral control.

The Ted Kaczynski connection is documented but more limited than often claimed. Kaczynski entered Harvard at 16 and participated (1959–1962) in a study led by Professor Henry Murray, who had worked for the OSS during WWII developing interrogation techniques. Murray described his own sessions as "vehement, sweeping, and personally abusive." Kaczynski spent approximately 200 hours in the study. **Whether Murray's specific study was MK-ULTRA-funded cannot be confirmed** — the relevant records were destroyed. Kaczynski himself disputed the connection from prison, writing that the experience "could not have been described as 'torture' even in the loosest sense of the word."

### Project Stargate: when the CIA investigated psychics

The U.S. government funded psychic research programs under various code names from **1972 to 1995**, spending approximately **$20 million**. This is confirmed by declassified CIA and DIA documents. The programs originated from concerns about Soviet "psychotronic" research (a 1972 classified report estimated the USSR was spending 60 million rubles annually on ESP research). Work was conducted primarily at Stanford Research Institute by parapsychologists Russell Targ and Harold Puthoff. Key participants included Ingo Swann (who coined "remote viewing"), Uri Geller (tested by CIA in August 1973), and Joseph McMoneagle (involved in approximately 450 missions).

The 1995 American Institutes for Research evaluation produced a split verdict: statistician Jessica Utts found a **statistically significant effect** (5–15% above chance); psychologist Ray Hyman deemed the findings premature. The review concluded "no remote viewing report ever provided actionable information for any intelligence operation." The CIA terminated and declassified the program. The government's interest was explicitly pragmatic — one CIA assessment noted they were interested not in whether Geller was "genuinely paranormal" but "whether his capabilities are exploitable by CIA."

### The Process Church: from Scientology to animal rescue

The Process Church of the Final Judgment, founded in 1966 London by ex-Scientologists Robert and Mary Ann de Grimston, taught the reconciliation of Christ and Satan as unified opposites. Their theology posited four deities (Jehovah, Lucifer, Satan, Christ) understood as archetypes. Despite the provocative theology, the group prohibited sex, drugs, and alcohol (only caffeine and nicotine permitted freely) and **did not practice magic**.

Ed Sanders' 1971 book *The Family* alleged Process Church connections to the Manson murders. **The Process Church sued for libel and won** — Dutton recalled and reprinted all copies with Process references deleted. The strongest documented connection: Process members visited Manson in prison and published an interview in their "Death Issue" magazine. Manson prosecutor Vincent Bugliosi examined the Process connection in *Helter Skelter* and found no prosecutable link. The group later transformed through several iterations and eventually became the **Best Friends Animal Society**, now one of the largest animal welfare organizations in the United States.

### The Finders: where documentation deliberately thins

On February 4, 1987, two well-dressed men were arrested in Tallahassee with six disheveled children (ages 2–11). The men belonged to the Finders, an intentional community in Washington, D.C. founded by Marion David Pettie, a former Air Force master sergeant. After the children's mothers (also Finders members) were located and confirmed they had consented to the trip, charges were dropped.

The case became controversial when U.S. Customs Agent Ramon Martinez wrote a report describing photographs of nude children and suspicious documents found during property searches. A 1993 report stated the D.C. police investigation had been dropped as a **"CIA internal matter."** The CIA acknowledged two connections: Pettie's wife Isabelle worked for the CIA from 1951/52 to 1961 (some documents say 1971), and a CIA-connected computer training company employed Finders members as part-time accountants. Pettie himself told *U.S. News & World Report* that "the CIA's interest in the Finders may stem from the fact that his late wife once worked for the Agency and that his son worked for a CIA proprietary firm, Air America."

The **2019 FBI Vault release** (324 pages, heavily redacted) confirmed Isabelle Pettie's CIA employment and her State Department–approved visas to North Korea, North Vietnam, and the Soviet Union. Multiple officers stated they experienced no overt CIA interference. But the documents contain no smoking gun, and no former Finders members or children have come forward with abuse claims. **The case exists in a deliberately created information vacuum** — enough documented intelligence connections to fuel suspicion, insufficient evidence to establish anything beyond those connections.

### The Franklin case: financial fraud meets irresolvable ambiguity

The Franklin Community Federal Credit Union was shut down in November 1988 with **$38–40 million missing**. Manager Lawrence King — a rising Republican operative who sang the National Anthem at the 1984 Republican National Convention — pleaded guilty to embezzlement and served approximately 10 years. This conviction is for financial fraud only.

During the investigation, multiple young adults alleged a child prostitution ring. A Douglas County grand jury in July 1990 called the allegations "a carefully crafted hoax" and indicted two accusers for perjury. Alisha Owen was convicted and sentenced to 9–15 years. **However**, Gary Caradori, the private investigator hired by the Nebraska legislative committee, died on July 11, 1990, when his small plane broke apart mid-air — the NTSB found no evidence of sabotage but Senator Loran Schmit submitted an affidavit stating he'd been warned Caradori's life was in danger.

The documentary *Conspiracy of Silence* was produced by Yorkshire Television for the Discovery Channel, then **pulled before its May 3, 1994 broadcast**. Discovery reimbursed production costs. Neither company officially commented. A rough edit was leaked and circulates online. The case resists definitive resolution: official investigations declared the allegations a hoax; DeCamp, investigative journalist Nick Bryant, and others argue for a cover-up. The evidence exists in genuine, perhaps permanent, ambiguity.

### Bohemian Grove: what journalists actually found

The Bohemian Club, founded in 1872 by San Francisco journalists, hosts an annual summer encampment for approximately 2,500 members at its 2,700-acre property in Monte Rio. Documented attendees include every Republican president since Calvin Coolidge, Henry Kissinger, David Rockefeller, Walter Cronkite, and Clarence Thomas. The most consequential documented event: a 1942 meeting that helped lay groundwork for Manhattan Project discussions.

The **Cremation of Care** ceremony dates to 1881 — a theatrical drama involving robed figures burning a coffin-like effigy of "Dull Care" before a 30–40 foot hollow concrete owl statue (designed by sculptor Haig Patigian in the 1920s). Walter Cronkite's recorded voice served for years as the voice of the Owl. The owl symbolizes wisdom, drawn from Athena's owl in Greek mythology — **no documented Club materials connect it to Moloch, Lilith, or other deities.**

Philip Weiss's 1989 *Spy Magazine* infiltration — widely regarded as the most detailed journalistic account — described heavy drinking, public urination, performances in drag, and ideology that was "right-wing, laissez-faire and quintessentially western, with some Druid tree worship thrown in for fun." Jon Ronson, who accompanied Alex Jones during his 2000 infiltration, wrote: "My lasting impression was of an all-pervading sense of immaturity: the Elvis impersonators, the pseudo-pagan spooky rituals, the heavy drinking." Jones claimed the ceremony was "ancient Canaanite, Luciferian, Babylon mystery religion" — an interpretation unsupported by the ceremony's documented 1881 theatrical origins or by any other journalist's account.

Sociologist G. William Domhoff (*The Bohemian Grove and Other Retreats*) characterized the Cremation of Care as scripted drama fostering elite camaraderie. **The documented facts are remarkable enough on their own**: a private club where presidents, Supreme Court justices, defense contractors, and media moguls gather for secret ceremonies in the redwoods. The conspiracy theories (Moloch worship, human sacrifice) are not supported by credible evidence.

## Thread 5: The steady pulse — lineage, technology, and what the pattern means

### The documented chain

The lineage from Golden Dawn through present practice is traceable, though the nature of each link varies. The **Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn** (est. 1888) included documented cultural elites — W.B. Yeats, Florence Farr, Arthur Machen — but functioned as a spiritual educational society rather than a vehicle for political power. **Aleister Crowley** joined in 1898, was expelled by 1900, and went on to found the A∴A∴ and lead the O.T.O. The strongest evidence for Crowley's intelligence work comes from Richard Spence's *Secret Agent 666* (2008): U.S. Army Military Intelligence Division files from WWI confirm American investigators discovered "Aleister Crowley was an employee of the British Government." MI5 admitted a Crowley file existed but had been "destroyed in the 1950s."

**Jack Parsons** received Crowley's Thelema through reading and the Agape Lodge. **LaVey** drew on Crowley's Enochian keys (among many other sources) but was not a direct organizational successor — the influence was textual, not initiatory. **Aquino** joined LaVey's Church of Satan, then split to form the Temple of Set, which was theistic where LaVey was atheistic and explicitly initiatory where LaVey was more theatrical. The chain is real but **the nature of each link differs**: some connections are organizational, some textual, some personal, and the metaphysical positions of each figure are often fundamentally incompatible with one another.

A parallel lineage worth noting: **Dion Fortune** (1890–1946), a Golden Dawn initiate who organized "The Magical Battle of Britain" during WWII — a series of group visualizations intended to formulate "seed ideas in the group mind of the race" to counter Nazi propaganda at a psychological-spiritual level. Her letters to order members are preserved and published. This represents perhaps the earliest documented explicit attempt to use occult practice as a form of counter-propaganda.

### Occult as technology: the Bernays parallel

The most analytically productive framework for understanding this material is not "do powerful people secretly worship Satan?" but rather: **how does the technology of perception management relate to the technology of ritual?** Aquino's framework provides the clearest articulation. His Lesser Black Magic explicitly encompassed politics and propaganda. His MindWar paper explicitly referenced "the esoteric arts of antiquity" alongside science and psychology. He treated magical practice as applied psychology focused on manipulating perceptions — which is precisely what PSYOP doctrine does.

But Aquino was not the first to articulate this parallel. **Edward Bernays** wrote in *Propaganda* (1928): "The conscious and intelligent manipulation of the organized habits and opinions of the masses is an important element in democratic society. Those who manipulate this unseen mechanism of society constitute an invisible government which is the true ruling power of our country." Bernays' "engineering of consent" used Freudian insights about unconscious desires to reshape behavior through symbolic action — the "Torches of Freedom" campaign reframing women's smoking as feminist liberation is a textbook case of what an occultist might call sympathetic magic translated into advertising. Walter Lippmann's *Public Opinion* (1922) had already established that the public operates on "pictures in their heads" rather than direct reality. Jacques Ellul's *Propaganda* (1962) argued that propaganda is an inescapable feature of technological society — and Aquino explicitly cited Ellul in the MindWar paper.

The structural parallel is real: **both occult ritual and modern propaganda systematically use symbols, narrative, and environmental manipulation to reshape consciousness in ways the target may not consciously recognize.** The difference is that propaganda theory developed from Enlightenment psychology and mass communication research, while occult practice draws on pre-modern initiatory traditions. Aquino is the documented figure who most explicitly bridged these two streams.

### Five interpretations and what the evidence supports

Occult symbolism in entertainment and power structures can represent: **(a)** genuine belief and practice — Parsons and Aquino clearly fall here; **(b)** deliberate psychological manipulation using "symbolic technology" — Bernays and PSYOP doctrine fall here, as does LaVey's atheistic framework; **(c)** cultural signaling among elites — the Golden Dawn's membership of cultural luminaries and the Bohemian Grove's elite networking suggest this function; **(d)** marketing/spectacle with no deeper significance — the Theda Bara phenomenon and LaVey's media operations exemplify this; or **(e)** some combination. The academic consensus, represented by scholars like Christopher Partridge ("occulture"), Erik Davis (*Techgnosis*), and Kennet Granholm, is that **all four operate simultaneously** in different contexts and sometimes within the same institution or individual.

What the evidence does *not* support is a unified, coordinated conspiracy. The documented record shows individual actors with varying motivations operating across overlapping but distinct spheres. The pattern is convergence, not coordination.

### "Revelation of the Method": where the trail enters conspiracy territory

The concept originates with **James Shelby Downard**, a marginal and enigmatic figure, and was systematized by **Michael A. Hoffman II** in *Secret Societies and Psychological Warfare* (1992). The concept proposes that a hidden elite deliberately reveals its methods of control as a form of psychological warfare — creating learned helplessness through awareness of manipulation without power to resist.

**Source quality warning**: Hoffman is a documented Holocaust denier and antisemite. His work mixes observations about propaganda with unfalsifiable conspiratorial frameworks and deeply problematic ideology. The concept as Downard and Hoffman articulate it — a deliberate, coordinated strategy by a unified "cryptocracy" — has no documented evidentiary basis.

However, the concept identifies a **real psychological phenomenon** through a paranoid lens. Documented examples of "methods being revealed" without consequence include: the declassification of MK-ULTRA files with minimal public accountability; Bernays literally publishing a book called *Propaganda* explaining his techniques; the Facebook Papers revealing known harms without meaningful reform. The intelligence concept of a **"limited hangout"** — deliberate partial release of information to control a narrative — is a documented technique. But these represent institutional dynamics and information management, not a coordinated occult strategy. The academic equivalents are far more modest in their claims: Chomsky and Herman's "manufacture of consent," Guy Debord's "spectacle," Peter Sloterdijk's "cynical reason" — frameworks describing how awareness of manipulation coexists with continued participation, without requiring a unified conspiatorial agent behind it.

## Conclusion: what the documented record reveals

Five findings emerge from tracing the documented evidence across these threads.

**First, the intersection is real but not unified.** Named individuals — Aquino, Parsons, Crowley — simultaneously participated in occult practice and military/industrial/intelligence work. These are not conspiracy theories; they are biographical facts attested by military records, institutional archives, and the participants' own published writings. But the evidence shows individuals with overlapping interests, not a coordinated program.

**Second, "occult" and "psychological operation" share a common grammar.** Both involve the systematic manipulation of symbols, environments, and perceptions to reshape consciousness. Aquino articulated this explicitly; Bernays practiced it without occult vocabulary; intelligence agencies investigated it through programs like Stargate. The technology of perception management is the connective tissue, regardless of the metaphysical framework wrapped around it.

**Third, self-mythologizing is as important as the underlying reality.** LaVey fabricated his biography. Anger fabricated much of *Hollywood Babylon*. Hollywood fabricated Theda Bara from whole cloth. Scientology fabricated Hubbard's role at the Parsonage. In this territory, the constructed narrative is often doing as much work as whatever reality lies beneath it — and sometimes the construction *is* the point. Distinguishing between the signal and the noise is the central epistemological challenge.

**Fourth, documentation deliberately thins at critical junctures.** Helms destroyed MK-ULTRA files. The Finders case was classified. Crowley's MI5 file was destroyed. Bohemian Grove enforces strict secrecy. The Franklin documentary was pulled. This pattern of deliberate opacity does not prove any particular theory about what lies behind it, but it does mean that **the absence of evidence is not evidence of absence** — it is sometimes evidence of destruction. Any honest account must acknowledge these gaps rather than filling them with either certainty or dismissal.

**Fifth, the most extraordinary facts in this territory are the documented ones.** A JPL co-founder performing sex magic with the future founder of Scientology while Aleister Crowley wrote frantic letters from England. A Lieutenant Colonel writing military PSYOP doctrine while leading an occult temple. The CIA spending two decades and $20 million testing psychics. Hollywood manufacturing an entire false identity complete with a hired phrenologist in 1915. These facts are stranger than most conspiracy theories — and they are real. The challenge for serious inquiry is not to embellish this material but to resist the temptation to make it tidier, more connected, or more sinister than the messy, contradictory documentary record actually shows.
