# Engage Encounter Axis

> `using-epi-logos` runs first. If it hasn't been invoked this turn, go there now.

`L4` and `L4'` form one shared encounter axis. The issue is lived on one side and observed on the other — and the work usually lives in the tension between them, not in either side alone.

Square B gives the main runtime entry surface:
- `L1`: causal structure
- `L4`: phenomenological disclosure
- `L4'`: scientific intervention
- `L1'`: phenomenal immediacy

## The Move

Before deeper analysis, map both sides:

1. How is the issue lived — what is disclosed from inside, what matters, what is at stake, what transformation of the knower is involved?
2. How is the issue observed — current state, desired state, constraints, evidence, candidate interventions, what would count as verification?
3. Where do the two sides reinforce, diverge, or distort each other?
4. What does the chosen topology require you to do with that tension?

## Topology Modulation

**In Torus:** foreground the side doing the live work, keep the other as a reality check. Don't overcomplicate a stable run.

**In Klein:** both sides must be stated explicitly. Look for reversals, inversions, mismatches. Let each side diagnose the other's blind spots.

**In Lemniscatic:** fold between both sides through `P4`. Track what changes each time the fold returns — what the earlier positions looked like before the fold, how they look different after.

## What to Avoid

- Treating the subjective side as optional because the task looks technical
- Treating the objective side as optional because the issue feels existential
- Pretending both sides agree when the disagreement is the real doorway

## Output

The map is analysis, not a template. Produce it as prose or structured notes — whatever serves the run. What it needs to show: how the issue is lived, how it is observed, where the real tension is, and what the topology requires next.
